## Two kinds of entry

Not every release fits a calendar. We publish both:

- **Monthly round-ups** — labelled by month, grouping that month's smaller additions, improvements and fixes into one entry.
- **Feature releases** — a larger piece of work gets its own entry, dated when it landed. These are the ones worth reading properly: a new pickface routing engine or a security settings page needs more than a bullet point.

A feature release is never repeated in that month's round-up, so nothing is double-counted.

## Finding a specific release

The contents panel on the right of the changelog lists every entry — each month and each named feature — so you can jump straight to a release instead of scrolling for it.

Each entry also has its own address. Right-click a contents link to copy it, and you can send someone directly to the release you mean rather than to the top of the page.

<Tip>Looking for a change in a particular area? The changelog is one page, so your browser's find (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>F</kbd>) searches the whole history at once — "pickface" or "invoice" will land you in the right entries.</Tip>

## Subscribing to updates

The changelog publishes an RSS feed at `/product-updates/changelog/rss.xml`. A new item appears when we add an entry, and when we add a section to an existing one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Copy the feed address">
    Open the [changelog](/product-updates/changelog) and use the RSS button at the top of the page, or take this site's address and append `/product-updates/changelog/rss.xml`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Into Slack">
    In any Slack channel, run `/feed subscribe` followed by the feed address. New releases are then posted to that channel automatically — useful for an internal operations or account management channel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Into email or a feed reader">
    Any RSS-to-email service works — point it at the same feed address. Feed readers take it directly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What we include

We write these for the people who use Clarus day to day, so the changelog covers what you can see and act on:

- New features and settings.
- Changes to how existing screens and processes behave.
- Fixes for faults that affected customers generally.
- Performance, security and compliance changes worth knowing about.

We leave out internal engineering work with no user-visible effect — infrastructure moves, framework upgrades, refactoring — and one-off data corrections for a single account. If a fix you reported affected only your data, your Clarus contact will confirm it directly rather than it appearing here.

<Info>Looking for how something works rather than when it changed? The [knowledgebase](/) is the reference; the changelog only tells you what moved and when.</Info>

## Telling us what to build

If something in these updates is close to what you need but not quite there, say so — most of what ships here started as a customer request. Raise it through the in-app help widget, or talk to your Clarus contact.
